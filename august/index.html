Investment memoFrom: Tal RosenbergNovember 2026Israeli early-stage fintech, public sources only

146 fintech companies, 1 memo. August, $500K at seed.

I built a screen for early-stage Israeli fintech in the vibe-coding era, when software is becoming a commodity, and ran it across 146 companies with AI agents and an independent audit. The question it asks first: which companies deliver the work itself, rather than a tool that helps people do it. Below is how the pool narrowed, and every check that was run on the company at the end of it.

AI agents for bank credit work
Score the business, not the source.

A specific company statement can support a 2. A 3 needs outside confirmation. A weak source lowers a separate confidence label, never the score.

Unknown is not zero.

Zero means I looked and it is not there. Unknown means it cannot be seen from outside. Merging them would punish companies that simply publish less.

Audit by rule, not by opinion.

A second agent checked all 930 scoring decisions against the written rules and changed 22, in both directions.

Competition

The leading commercial lending system launched Analyst Digital Partner in April 2026 and says it cuts review effort by 60% to 70%. EnFi raised $15M in February 2026 for credit-analyst agents. Large banks are building their own.

Team and regulation

No banking founder, and model risk rules

Neither founder has held a credit or regulatory role. US banks must validate any model that drives credit decisions, which lengthens sales and demands a decision trail. The fix is a credit hire, not a pivot.

Decision

at seed

After a first meeting, and only if 3 things check out: who the bank is and what it pays, what the reported $800K actually is, and how the product passes a bank's model risk review.

"Software helps a credit analyst. August claims to do the analyst's work."That is what puts it against a labour budget rather than a software budget. Its own line is "banking's last piece of software": the system of action above the loan origination system, not one more screen inside it.

Method

How the numbers were built

The 2 calculations the memo rests on, so anyone can rerun them.

Pricing the outcome

No price is published, so the table is anchored on the single reported deployment: about $800K a year for a large bank. $500,000 at an assumed $20M post-money price is 2.5% at entry and 1.25% at exit after 50% dilution. Exits at 4x to 8x ARR. Weighting the 4 scenarios by their probabilities gives an expected return of about 2.7x.

Sizing the labour budget

64,390 US credit analysts (BLS, May 2025) multiplied by the median wage of $83,510 gives about $5.4B a year. It leaves out relationship managers, servicing and risk staff, and outsourcing, so it is a floor, not a forecast.

Limits

What this cannot do

It is a filter for which conversations are worth having, not a substitute for having them.

Public sources only

No data room, no founder meeting, no customer calls. The bank and the revenue are company claims until a single call confirms them.

An incomplete map of the market

Companies in stealth, founded in the last few months, or never listed anywhere are missing. Most founding years and amounts raised rest on aggregator sites rather than the company register.
